# CROP: Conservative Reward for Model-based Offline Policy Optimization (Li et al., 2023)

**Authors:** Yihao Li, Zijian Zhou, Mingyuan Li et al. · **Venue:** preprint (revised April 2026) · **arXiv:** 2310.17245

## What it is

CROP applies CQL-style conservative penalization directly to a learned REWARD estimator inside a
model-based offline RL pipeline — rather than to the Q-function as CQL does. The reward head is
trained to simultaneously minimize standard prediction error on dataset transitions AND suppress its
predicted reward on random (OOD) action samples. Because Q-values are computed by rolling through the
(conservative) reward model, the conservatism propagates into the value function without needing an
explicit Q-regularizer. CROP provides theoretical guarantees that the resulting Q lower-bounds the true
policy value under the behavior distribution.

## Why it matters here

CROP is the closest published instantiation of the conservative reward head we need for exp0047. Our
specific problem is a WM reward estimator that assigns inflated predictions to OOD 2-step sequences
(the exp0045 finding: ~12% of random sequences outscore the true gesture). CROP names this failure
mode precisely and provides the training-time fix: add a push-down loss on random actions to the
standard MSE regression. This directly maps to our `reward_conservative_coef` implementation.

## Details (method depth)

### The CROP objective

The CROP reward-head training loss combines two terms:

```
L_CROP(R_hat) =  E_{(s,a,r)~D}[(R_hat(s,a) − r)²]          ← standard MSE on data
               + α · E_{s~D, a~μ}[R_hat(s,a)]               ← push DOWN on OOD actions
```

where μ is typically uniform-random over the action space. The first term fits the reward head to
real transitions. The second term penalizes the absolute predicted reward on random actions — driving
the head to output low values on inputs outside the data support.

**Key difference from CQL**: CQL's regularizer pushes DOWN on OOD actions and simultaneously pushes
UP on data actions (the contrastive pair). CROP drops the explicit push-UP on data actions: the
standard MSE already pulls in-distribution estimates toward true rewards; the push-UP is redundant.
This simplification also avoids the risk of over-fitting the push-UP to noisy reward labels.

### Conservative Q lower-bound (theoretical result)

CROP shows that if R_hat is trained under L_CROP with sufficiently large α, then for any policy π:

```
Q^π_CROP(s,a)  ≤  Q^π(s,a)   for all (s,a) in the data support
```

The proof proceeds by showing the conservative reward induces conservative Bellman targets, and the
resulting Q is a lower bound under standard occupancy arguments. The bound tightness depends on α
(larger α → tighter lower bound but more aggressive suppression of in-distribution estimates).

### OOD push-down target: uniform-random vs replay-supported

A key design question (open in the paper): what distribution μ to use for the OOD action samples?

- **Uniform-random** (paper's default): simple, broad coverage, guarantees no action is missed.
  Risk: in high-dimensional action spaces, uniform may place no mass near actually exploitable
  near-OOD actions (ones the planner is close to selecting).
- **Replay-supported OOD**: sample actions from a distribution supported by the replay buffer but NOT
  matched to the current (s,a) pair — e.g., actions from OTHER states in the batch. These may be
  more realistic near-OOD cases but reduce to an implicit data-augmentation scheme.

The paper evaluates uniform-random on D4RL continuous control (dense rewards). Our exp0047 uses
uniform-random actions sampled from the **detached belief state** (the current belief after
encode+GRU, detached so the OOD penalty only shapes the reward head and not the representation).
Whether belief-conditioned or truly uniform is better for sparse rewards is open.

### Dense-reward D4RL eval vs our sparse setting

CROP is evaluated on D4RL offline RL benchmarks (MuJoCo locomotion: HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2D,
Ant). These are dense-reward continuous-control tasks. Two open questions for our setting:

1. **Sparse reward**: in D4RL most transitions have nonzero reward, so the MSE term provides a strong
   pull-UP across the dataset. In our RTFM task, ~95% of transitions have r=0; the MSE term provides
   almost no pull-UP signal, and the push-DOWN term is operating in a near-vacuum of positive signal.
   This could mean: (a) α must be smaller (the push-DOWN is already strong relative to MSE), or (b)
   the one-sided relu variant (clamp negative OOD predictions, penalize only positives) is safer.

2. **Action space**: D4RL uses continuous multidimensional actions. Our actions are discrete (n_act
   categories). Uniform-random is trivially defined and cheap to sample in the discrete case.

### Our online variant (exp0047)

exp0047 implements a deliberate online variant of CROP: the OOD set is not a frozen offline dataset
complement but the *current replay's complement* — M=16 random actions sampled per step at WM-train
time. The one-sided relu clamp is added as a sparse-reward adaptation:

```python
cons = rew(belief.detach()[:,None,:].expand(b,M,·), randint(0, n_act, (b,M))).clamp_min(0).mean()
loss_wm += α · cons
```

The `.clamp_min(0)` ensures we penalize only overestimates (positive predicted reward on random
actions), not underestimates — avoiding suppression of the genuine zero-reward signal. The `belief.detach()`
ensures the conservative penalty flows gradient only through the reward head, not through the encoder
or GRU belief state.

## Open questions

- **Sparse-reward regime**: Does CROP's theory generalize to near-zero-reward datasets where the
  MSE term provides minimal in-distribution pull? Is the one-sided relu variant theoretically sound
  (does it still lower-bound Q)?
- **α schedule**: The paper uses fixed α. Given our curriculum (length-1 → length-2 switch), the
  balance between MSE and push-DOWN signal changes; an α schedule or Lagrangian dual may be needed.
- **Interaction with aux losses**: exp0047 also uses the Dynalang masked-manual auxiliary loss.
  Whether the CROP penalty interacts with the aux loss through the shared belief representation is
  unstudied (detaching the belief prevents direct gradient coupling, but shared features may still
  carry indirect pressure).
- **Push-down target**: uniform-random vs replay-supported OOD — the paper does not ablate this.

## Relevance to our work

CROP is the direct literature anchor for exp0047. The connection chain:

- **exp0025** ([[0025-reward-head-patch]]): two-hot distributional head bounded reward MAGNITUDE (the
  prior bounded head; no OOD push-down). Local precedent for reward-head surgery.
- **exp0045** ([[0045-rtfm-oracle-probe]]): oracle probe measured the specific CROP-relevant failure:
  oracle pct ≈ 0.88 (12% OOD false positives lift random sequences above the true gesture). This is
  EXACTLY the OOD overestimation CROP targets.
- **exp0046** ([[0046-rtfm-robust-planning]]): planning-time averaging (K=10) is a partial fix
  (pct 0.88→0.92). CROP motivates the training-time approach as the root fix — "the head is starved,
  so training-time regularization, not planning-time discounting."
- **exp0047** ([[0047-rtfm-conservative-reward]]): implements CROP's core idea in an online MBRL
  setting: push-down on random OOD actions at WM-train time, one-sided relu for sparse rewards,
  belief detached so only the reward head is shaped. The `reward_conservative_coef` (α=3.0) and
  `reward_conservative_samples` (M=16) are the CROP knobs. A successful outcome (oracle pct → ~1.0,
  MPC correct lifts off floor) would validate CROP-style training-time conservatism in sparse,
  discrete, online MBRL — a setting the paper does not cover.

See also [[cql-2020]] for the foundational OOD conservatism mechanism CROP extends to reward heads.
