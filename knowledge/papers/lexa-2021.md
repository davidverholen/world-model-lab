# Discovering and Achieving Goals via World Models — LEXA (Mendonca, Rybkin, Daniilidis, Hafner & Pathak, NeurIPS 2021)

**Lab:** UC Berkeley / Penn / DeepMind · **arXiv:** 2110.09514 · **Venue:** NeurIPS 2021 · **Project:** https://orybkin.github.io/lexa/ · **Read state:** abstract depth + project page + NeurIPS proceedings page (full PDF not fetched; achiever training details, rollout length, and imagination trustworthiness not formula-verified)

## What it is

LEXA (Latent Explorer Achiever) decomposes the unsupervised goal-reaching problem into two policies over a **shared Dreamer (RSSM) world model**: an **explorer** that discovers novel latent states via foresight-guided planning, and an **achiever** that trains *exclusively in imagination* to reach those discovered states as goals. After unsupervised pre-training with no task reward, the achiever can zero-shot solve tasks specified as goal images across 40 test tasks in robotic manipulation and locomotion. The explorer uses the world model to plan ahead and identify states that would be surprising — a forward-looking alternative to retrospective novelty that searches the replay buffer. Both policies train entirely from imagined rollouts; no real-environment interaction is required during the achiever's learning phase.

## Why it matters here

LEXA's explorer/achiever split over a shared RSSM world model is **structurally identical to our exp0066 design** for the multi-step gesture chain:

| LEXA (unsupervised goal-reaching) | Our exp0066 (multi-step gesture chain) |
|---|---|
| Explorer discovers surprising latent states | Real-env collection discovers confirmed prefix-complete latents (step k-1 done) |
| Achiever trains in imagination to reach explorer-found states | Actor-critic trains in imagination seeded from buffered prefix-complete latents |
| Shared Dreamer RSSM world model | Our ConditionedRSSM (same family) |
| Goal = latent representation of target image | Goal = "complete step k" from a buffered step-(k-1)-complete latent |
| Achiever never needs real-env access during training | Imagination-seeded actor-critic never needs real-env to reach step k during training |

The Dreamer / RSSM lineage means **LEXA's patterns transfer directly to our stack**: the achiever training setup (imagined rollouts seeded from goal latents) is architecturally compatible with our ConditionedRSSM actor-critic. LEXA is the existence proof that this split works at non-trivial scale and robustness (40 tasks, multi-object sequential manipulation).

**Three design takeaways for exp0066:**

1. **Explorer-found states as achiever training targets is a proven pattern.** LEXA demonstrates that collecting frontier states (here: states that would be surprising) and then training an achiever to reach them in imagination is a complete, working system. The exp0066 analog uses the replay buffer as the "explorer" (real-env collection naturally discovers prefix-complete latents over time) and the imagination actor-critic as the "achiever." This is simpler than LEXA's explicit explorer policy; the question is whether the replay buffer accumulates enough prefix-complete examples to fuel the achiever's training.

2. **The achiever trains IN IMAGINATION, not in the real environment.** This is load-bearing: LEXA's achiever never interacts with the real environment while learning to reach goals. The imagined rollouts are the only training signal. This is exactly exp0066's design — we seed imagination from real-latent checkpoints but the actor learns purely from the imagined trajectory. LEXA validates that imagination-only achiever training produces real-environment competence.

3. **Foresight-guided explorer vs retrospective replay.** LEXA's key innovation over prior work (LEXA's abstract explicitly contrasts with "retrospective exploration that searches the replay buffer") is the explorer using foresight: it queries the world model for novel *future* states rather than selecting past replay states. For exp0066, our "explorer" is currently retrospective — we buffer past prefix-complete latents. LEXA raises the question of whether a foresight-based approach (imagine forward from the current state to find latents that are one step past the current frontier, before they are actually observed) could accelerate the curriculum. This is worth tracking as an escalation if the retrospective approach is too sparse.

**Relevance to [[0065-rtfm-per-step-plateau-large-budget]] and [[rtfm-rollout-perf]]:** LEXA's achiever training is directly the imagination-rollout loop we already run (DreamerV3-style actor-critic in RSSM imagination). The question in our setting is whether seeding from prefix-complete latents (instead of random seeds) gives the actor the right distribution to learn step k. LEXA says yes — but LEXA's goals are *images* with a continuous task space; our "step k" is a discrete gesture, and the achiever needs to learn a conditioned policy, not just reach any high-novelty state.

## Details

**Architecture (abstract depth).** LEXA builds on the Dreamer RSSM framework:
- **World model:** Standard DreamerV3 RSSM trained from image observations via RSSM dynamics + VQVAE-like latent states (or continuous stochastic latents — not confirmed at abstract depth which Dreamer version is used; the paper was concurrent with DreamerV2).
- **Explorer policy:** A policy trained with intrinsic reward derived from the world model's *prediction of future surprise* — it imagines forward and selects actions leading to states the model predicts will be novel. This is foresight-guided, not retrospective.
- **Achiever policy:** Goal-conditioned on a target latent state s_g (discovered by the explorer and stored). Trained via imagined rollouts: sample s_g from the explorer's buffer, seed imagination from the current state, roll out the achiever, compute reward = similarity of reached latent to s_g, backpropagate through imagination via Dreamer's actor-critic machinery.
- **Goal representation:** Target states s_g are represented as latents (not raw images), consistent with our RSSM latent goals.

**Imagination trustworthiness (abstract depth, not formula-verified).** LEXA trains the achiever exclusively in imagination. The paper does not discuss a compounding-error bound in the abstract or project page. The implicit assumption is that the RSSM world model is accurate enough over the achiever's planning horizon that imagination-trained policies transfer to the real environment. This transfer is demonstrated empirically on 40 tasks — evidence that imagination is trustworthy *at the rollout lengths LEXA uses*. The rollout length is not specified in the abstract; DreamerV3 typically uses H=15 imagination steps. If our step-k completion requires fewer steps (length-2 gestures = 2 actions), this is comfortably within the trust range.

**Multi-object sequential manipulation results (abstract-level).** LEXA can solve goals requiring interacting with multiple objects in sequence — directly relevant to our gesture chaining. This is the strongest evidence that imagination-trained achievers generalize to compositional tasks, not just single-step goals.

**Scope note.** LEXA is designed for unsupervised goal discovery in visual RL. Our setting differs: goals are not discovered by exploration but *defined by the manual* (step k is the next gesture in the recipe). This means the "explorer" phase is simpler for us (the manual specifies the frontier), but the achiever training remains identical in structure.

## Open questions

- What rollout length does LEXA's achiever use? If it is H=15 (standard Dreamer), is that enough for length-2 gesture chains? (Likely yes, but confirm before exp0066 design is finalized.)
- How does LEXA handle the non-stationarity introduced by the explorer continuously pushing new goal states into the achiever's training distribution? Is there a priority or curriculum over goal states, or flat uniform sampling from the explorer's buffer?
- LEXA's explorer uses foresight — imagining forward to find novel future states before they are observed. For exp0066, could a foresight-based approach generate prefix-complete latents *in imagination* without waiting for the replay buffer to accumulate them? (Risk: imagination-of-imagination compounds model error; see ROMI's compounding-error analysis.)
- LEXA uses a goal-conditioned achiever reward based on latent similarity. Our reward is a gesture-completeness flag from the real environment (sparse). Does the latent-similarity reward in LEXA implicitly solve the credit-assignment problem that our sparse reward cannot?
