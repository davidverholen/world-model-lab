# VIME: Variational Information Maximizing Exploration (Houthooft et al., NeurIPS 2016)

**Authors:** Rein Houthooft, Xi Chen, Yan Duan, John Schulman, Filip De Turck, Pieter Abbeel · **Lab:** Ghent University / UC Berkeley · **Venue:** NeurIPS 2016 · **arXiv:** 1605.09674

## What it is

VIME is an exploration method that computes an intrinsic reward equal to the **information gain** the agent obtains about its dynamics model after observing a transition. Rather than rewarding prediction error (high when the model is wrong) or prediction accuracy (high when the model is right in predictable states), VIME rewards *how much a transition updated the model's beliefs* — a signal that is high precisely when the transition was genuinely surprising to a well-calibrated model and low when the state is either trivially predictable or irreducibly stochastic. The dynamics model is a Bayesian neural network (BNN) approximated with variational inference; the intrinsic reward is the KL divergence between the posterior over model parameters after seeing the transition and the prior before it.

## Why it matters here

VIME provides the **single-source information-gain framing** that the exp0048 validated-reading reward extends contrastively. In VIME, the reward measures how much the observed transition taught the model about dynamics. In our mechanism, the reward measures how much *more* the manual-conditioned model learned than the null-manual model — i.e. the manual's marginal information gain, validated against the real transition. VIME is the direct mathematical ancestor of the r_intrinsic formula in [[validated-reading-reward]].

## Details (method depth)

### The information-gain intrinsic reward

Let φ denote the parameters of the BNN dynamics model. Before seeing transition (s_t, a_t, s_{t+1}), the posterior over φ is q(φ | ξ_{t-1}) where ξ_{t-1} is the agent's experience history. After seeing the transition, the posterior updates to q(φ | ξ_t). The intrinsic reward is:

```
r_intrinsic(s_t, a_t) = KL[ q(φ | ξ_t) || q(φ | ξ_{t-1}) ]
```

This is the information gain: how many bits the new transition added to the agent's model of dynamics. The KL is taken over the **parameter space**, not the observation space — the model learns what *kind of dynamics* the environment has, not just what specific transitions occur.

The total reward is then r_total = r_extrinsic + η * r_intrinsic where η is a scalar weight.

### BNN posterior approximation

Maintaining an exact posterior over neural-network parameters is intractable. VIME uses **mean-field variational inference**: the posterior is factored as q(φ) = Π_i q(φ_i) with each factor a diagonal Gaussian N(μ_i, σ_i²). The variational parameters (μ, σ) are updated by gradient descent on the ELBO after each new transition, giving a tractable approximate posterior.

The **KL between posteriors** is then approximated in closed form (both are diagonal Gaussians) without sampling:

```
KL[ q(φ | ξ_t) || q(φ | ξ_{t-1}) ] ≈ Σ_i [ log(σ_{i,old}/σ_{i,new}) + (σ_{i,new}² + (μ_{i,new} - μ_{i,old})²) / (2σ_{i,old}²) - 1/2 ]
```

In practice the update is approximated by running a small number of SGVB (reparameterization trick) gradient steps on the new transition after each environment step, then computing the KL before and after. This is the main computational cost of VIME: one BNN update per real environment step.

### Why it is an information-gain signal, not a prediction-error signal

This is the key distinction from [[icm-2017]]:

- **ICM** (foil): intrinsic reward = prediction error in feature space. High when the model is wrong. Maximizing it drives the agent to states where its model fails — which includes genuinely novel dynamics (good) but also irreducibly stochastic / noisy-TV states (bad).
- **VIME**: intrinsic reward = KL between posteriors = how much the model *updated*. A transition in a state the model already understands well produces a small update (small KL) even if there's high instantaneous prediction error; a genuinely novel dynamic produces a large update. Crucially, irreducible aleatoric noise also produces small updates once the model has learned the noise distribution — the model stops updating on random noise because its epistemic uncertainty can't be reduced.

In short: ICM rewards the model being wrong; VIME rewards the model *learning*. These come apart in noisy or predictable states.

### Dark-room / noisy-TV properties

- **Dark room** (all states boring and predictable): a static room has no dynamics to learn → after initial exploration the BNN posterior stabilizes → KL ≈ 0 → intrinsic reward ≈ 0 → agent eventually leaves. Partial fix (not complete: the agent may still linger initially).
- **Noisy TV**: in an environment with pure aleatoric noise, the BNN eventually learns the noise *distribution* (e.g. "this screen flickers uniformly") → posterior stops updating → KL → 0 → reward → 0. This is a meaningful improvement over ICM, but requires the BNN to distinguish aleatoric from epistemic uncertainty (diagonal Gaussian is a weak approximation here; the model may underfit the noise and keep treating it as epistemic uncertainty).

The remaining limitation is compute: a BNN update per step is expensive at scale.

### Benchmark results (arxiv:1605.09674)

VIME was evaluated on MuJoCo locomotion tasks (SwimmerGather, AntGather) and discrete-action maze tasks. Key finding: VIME's information-gain reward produced substantially faster exploration than random exploration and entropy-based bonuses (REINFORCE+H) on tasks with sparse extrinsic reward, because the agent actively sought novel dynamics rather than novel observations.

## Open questions

- **BNN update cost**: the per-step variational update is prohibitive in modern GPU-batch training. More efficient approximations (Stein variational gradient descent, last-layer Bayesian heads, ensemble disagreement as a surrogate) are needed for our setting.
- **Aleatoric vs epistemic split**: diagonal-Gaussian mean-field is a weak model of uncertainty. Genuinely irreducible stochasticity may be slow to suppress if the BNN underfits its noise model.
- **Parameter-space vs prediction-space KL**: the parameter-space KL is principled but indirect. A prediction-space approximation (expected prediction-error reduction) is cheaper and used by Oudeyer-Kaplan learning progress; the relationship between the two formulations is an open theoretical question.

## Relevance to our work

### The exp0048 connection

The validated-reading reward in [[validated-reading-reward]] / [[0048-rtfm-validated-reading]] is VIME's information-gain signal applied **contrastively across the manual condition**:

```
r_intrinsic(s, a, s'_real) = clip_≥0(  pred_error(s, a, s'_real | NULL manual)
                                      − pred_error(s, a, s'_real | correct manual) )
```

Instead of measuring how much a transition updated *the* model (VIME, single-source), we measure how much *more* the correct manual helped than no manual (marginal IG). The real transition validates both predictions against the environment's true response.

**What VIME is missing vs our mechanism:**
1. **No language / reading**: VIME's dynamics model is conditioned on (s, a) only. There is no text, no manual, no grounding question. The information-gain signal rewards novel dynamics, not manual-following.
2. **Single-source, not contrastive**: VIME has one model; our mechanism requires at minimum two forward passes (with-manual vs null-manual). The gap between them is what rewards reading specifically.
3. **Not marginal**: VIME's KL rewards learning about dynamics in general. Our mechanism rewards only the marginal contribution of the manual — in states where the manual is uninformative, the marginal gain is zero even if the dynamics are novel.

### Relationship to the foil and anti-wirehead pattern

- **[[icm-2017]]** (foil): ICM rewards prediction ERROR — the inverted sign. An agent following ICM's signal would be rewarded when its model was wrong, which is the opposite of "manuals that predict reality correctly." ICM is the clearest contrast to our mechanism.
- **[[rnd-2018]]** (anti-wirehead pattern): RND's key property is that the reward target is a frozen random network the agent cannot control. Our reward is gated on the real environment's response — also un-fakeable by the agent. The same anti-wireheading logic applies: the agent cannot make the real environment match a prediction it didn't actually derive from the manual.
