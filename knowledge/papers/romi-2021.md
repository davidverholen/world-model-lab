# Offline Reinforcement Learning with Reverse Model-based Imagination — ROMI (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2021)

**Lab:** IIIS, Tsinghua University · **arXiv:** 2110.00188 · **Venue:** NeurIPS 2021 · **Read state:** abstract depth (abstract + NeurIPS proceedings page + multiple secondary sources; full PDF not fetched — compounding-error theorem cited as claimed, not formula-verified)

## What it is

ROMI is an offline RL framework that learns a **reverse dynamics model** — a model that predicts a predecessor state given a current state and a "backward" action — and pairs it with a **backtracking rollout policy** that generates imagined trajectories *leading backward toward target states* in the offline dataset. The resulting synthetic trajectories are used as additional training data for a standard model-free offline RL algorithm (e.g., TD3+BC), augmenting the dataset with states that bridge the distribution gap between the offline data support and the regions the forward policy needs to reach. ROMI achieves state-of-the-art performance on D4RL offline benchmarks while generating more conservative behaviors than forward-model-based offline RL approaches that suffer from over-generalization in out-of-support regions.

## Why it matters here

ROMI is the **closest published analog to our exp0066 backward-imagination curriculum** for the multi-step gesture chain. The structural parallel:

| ROMI (offline RL) | Our exp0066 (online RSSM) |
|---|---|
| Reverse dynamics model p̃(sₜ₋₁ \| sₜ, ã) | Reverse rollout seeded from buffered prefix-complete RSSM latent |
| Target states = high-return states in offline dataset | Target states = buffered latents where step k-1 is confirmed complete |
| Backward imagined rollout → augments offline dataset | Backward-seeded imagination → actor-critic trains on the tail (steps k…N) |
| Bridges distribution gap in offline data | Manufactures deep-step experience that real-env exploration reaches only ~p^(k-1) of the time |
| Conservative: stays near offline support | Conservative: stays near real-trajectory prefix latents (the "frontier latents") |

The key conceptual delta: ROMI targets **OOD coverage** (filling gaps in an offline dataset between low-return and high-return regions), while exp0066 targets **chain ordering** (manufacturing the rare experience of being in a step-k-ready state so the actor can learn step k). Both use backward imagination to reach a "target" that forward exploration rarely reaches. The mechanism is isomorphic; the motivation differs.

**Three design takeaways for exp0066:**

1. **Reverse dynamics model is a real, trainable object.** ROMI demonstrates that a neural reverse dynamics model p̃(sₜ₋₁ | sₜ, ã) can be trained stably on offline data and used for multi-step rollouts. This validates the existence-proof concern ("can you actually learn a reliable backward model?") before we commit to exp0066's design.

2. **Backtracking policy + reverse model as a system.** ROMI does not just run the reverse dynamics model open-loop; it trains a **separate backtracking rollout policy** to select which "backward actions" ã to take to stay close to the data manifold. Our analog is the seeding discipline: rather than a trained backtracking policy, we seed rollouts directly from buffered frontier latents (a simpler, cleaner choice because our latents *are already on the real-trajectory manifold by construction*). The ROMI lesson: we do not need a trained backward policy if the seed is a real observed state.

3. **Compounding-error analysis bounds how far backward you can trust.** ROMI includes a theoretical analysis of how backward imagination error compounds with rollout depth (full PDF, not formula-verified at abstract depth). The qualitative conclusion, corroborated by MBPO (arxiv:1906.08253) which proves a monotonic-improvement bound only for short rollouts branched from real states, is: **multi-step backward imagination accumulates model error like forward imagination, so the walk-back should be gradual (one step at a time) and always seeded from real observed states, not from previously imagined states.** ROMI's offline setting constrains this automatically (targets are in the dataset); our setting enforces it by construction (we only seed from buffered real-trajectory prefix latents, never from imagination-of-imagination). The "imagination is only trustworthy one step past the frontier" caveat in [[hierarchical-imagination-agent]] §3a is consistent with ROMI's analysis.

**The walk-back schedule implication:** ROMI's compounding-error argument (and the MBPO branching bound) jointly imply that for exp0066, the curriculum should walk back **one step at a time** from confirmed frontier latents, not attempt multi-step backward leaps. The reason: a one-step backward seed has model error ~O(ε) (RSSM single-step error); a k-step backward seed constructed by iterating the reverse model has error ~O(kε) or worse if errors are multiplicative. Since our RSSM is not a reverse model and we are seeding from real buffered latents (not imagining backward), exp0066 sidesteps this error entirely — but the analysis supports **not** implementing a learned reverse dynamics model in the RSSM and instead relying on real-latent seeding.

## Details

**Architecture (abstract depth).** ROMI trains two components jointly: (1) a reverse dynamics model p̃_θ(sₜ₋₁ | sₜ, ã) — analogous to an inverse dynamics model but predicting the *predecessor* state rather than the action — and (2) a backtracking rollout policy π̃_φ(ã | sₜ) that selects backward actions to stay near the offline data support. At rollout time: starting from a high-return state s_T in the offline dataset, the backtracking policy selects ã, the reverse model predicts s_{T-1}, and the process is iterated to produce a backward trajectory (..., s_{T-2}, s_{T-1}, s_T). The trajectory is then flipped, relabeled with rewards from the forward reward model, and added to the offline training buffer.

**Conservatism mechanism.** Unlike forward model-based offline RL (MOPO, MOReL, COMBO), ROMI achieves conservatism *by construction*: backward rollouts from dataset target states always move toward already-observed states. The forward model-based approaches require explicit uncertainty penalties (MOPO: r̃ = r̂ - λ·std) or pessimistic absorbing states (MOReL) to stay in-support; ROMI does not (claims the abstract). This maps onto our design: seeding from buffered real-env latents is inherently conservative for the same reason.

**Benchmark results (abstract-level claim).** State-of-the-art on D4RL locomotion and manipulation benchmarks (HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d, Ant, and pen/door/hammer/relocate; exact numbers not extracted at abstract depth).

**Offline RL vs our online setting.** ROMI is trained on a fixed offline dataset; it does not interact with the environment. Exp0066 runs online with a live RSSM that updates every round. This means: (a) our "offline dataset" is the replay buffer, updated continuously — the frontier latents are always current; (b) the RSSM's reverse model does not need to be trained (we use real latents, not learned backward rollouts); (c) the distribution-gap problem ROMI addresses maps to our per-round frontier-latent availability (steps k-1 are rare, ROMI shows backward imagination can substitute).

## Open questions

- Does ROMI's compounding-error theorem give an explicit O(f(k)) bound (linear, quadratic, exponential?) — or only a qualitative argument? This is the missing piece at abstract read depth; the answer would sharpen the walk-back schedule for exp0066.
- ROMI learns a dedicated reverse dynamics model; we rely on real-latent seeding. Does the quality of our RSSM's single-step forward model imply anything about how many steps past the frontier a single imagined trajectory can be trusted? (MBPO says ~1–5 steps for MuJoCo dynamics; our discrete RSSM may be more or less reliable.)
- ROMI's backtracking policy trains on offline data. If we ever wanted to implement a true backward RSSM curriculum (imagining backward from a future goal state rather than seeding from past real latents), ROMI is the architecture to follow. Worth revisiting if exp0066's real-latent seeding hits a data sparsity wall.
